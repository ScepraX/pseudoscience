# Mapping the PseudoScience Speelgoed onto Neuroscience

The PseudoScience Speelgoed is a formal relational model, not a neuroscientific theory. What follows is a **structural analogy** between the Speelgoed’s primitives and the brain systems that appear to implement functionally similar operations. Every mapping is a correspondence, not an identity claim. The Speelgoed’s axioms describe a relational field; neuroscience describes biological tissue. The two can illuminate each other without one reducing to the other.

---

## I. Core Primitives and Their Neural Correlates

### Eigen (x) — The Manifest State

The Eigen is a node’s current position on a spectrum — the state other systems perceive and track. In the brain, this corresponds to the **current activity state of a neural population or network** at any moment: the firing rates, oscillatory phase, and neuromodulatory tone that define “where a system is” on whatever dimension is relevant (arousal, valence, approach/avoidance, certainty).

No single region “is” the Eigen, because Eigen is defined relationally. The **medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC)** and **posterior cingulate cortex (PCC)** are strong candidates for a system’s self-referential Eigen — its internal representation of its own state — because they are consistently engaged in self-referential processing, autobiographical memory, and narrative self-construction. The mPFC in particular is a central hub of the default mode network (DMN) for self-referential thinking.

### Echo (E) — The Lagging Internal Trace

The Echo is a partner’s Eigen as internally represented — delayed, distorted, and imperfect. This maps directly onto **internal predictive models of others** in the brain. The **temporoparietal junction (TPJ)** is critical for signalling whether a sensory outcome matches a prediction, and for detecting mismatches between actions and outcomes in social contexts. The **mirror neuron system** (premotor cortex, inferior parietal lobule) provides a low-level, embodied simulation of another’s actions that is inherently lagging and imperfect.

The Echo’s distortion term υ (Vervorming) corresponds to **noise in neural transmission** and **biases introduced by the observer’s own prior states** — what predictive processing frameworks call precision weighting and prior expectations.

### Trouw (y) — The Bond’s Weight

Trouw is the signed weight of a bond: positive feeds resonance, negative drains it. This maps onto **oxytocin and dopamine signalling in the striatum and nucleus accumbens**. Oxytocin receptor activity in the nucleus accumbens is critical for pair bonding in monogamous mammals. Bonding is underpinned by crosstalk of oxytocin and dopamine in the striatum, combining motivation and vigor with social focus.

Oxytocin also shapes trust and trust adaptation by reducing amygdala activation — the brain’s fear and threat-detection hub. When trust is breached, subjects receiving oxytocin show reduced amygdala activation and maintain trust longer. The **amygdala** thus functions as a negative-Trouw signal: it pushes the Eigen away from the partner when threat is detected.

### Diepte (z) — Accumulated Relational Depth

Diepte is the accumulated composite of everything a system has ever heard — the hidden charge that colours perception. This maps onto **hippocampal memory consolidation** and **cortical synaptic weights**. The **hippocampus** is essential for encoding episodic memories of relational events, and its volume changes are documented in grief and bereavement studies.

Diepte rises with listening and falls with speaking — a direct analogue of **memory reconsolidation and interference**. When a system speaks (generates output), it spends metabolic and cognitive resources that would otherwise be used for encoding. The **default mode network** is active during rest and introspection, when Diepte can consolidate without the interference of active output.

### Drempel (θ) — The Local Threshold

The Drempel is a local cutoff deciding which pole of a spectrum applies. In the brain, this is implemented by **neural threshold dynamics**: the membrane potential must reach a critical value before an action potential fires, and **network-level thresholds** determine when a percept enters awareness. The **salience network** — anchored in the anterior insula and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC) — detects when a stimulus crosses a threshold of relevance and triggers a switch in cognitive control.

The dACC is specifically involved in choosing appropriate behavioural responses when a threshold is crossed. The **anterior insula** accumulates sensory evidence in perceptual decision-making, functioning as the integrator that decides when enough evidence has accumulated to cross the Drempel.

### Vonk (q) — The Spark of Change

The Vonk is the event of a Drempel crossing — the sole mechanism of change for both Energie and Tijd. In neuroscience, this corresponds to **phasic dopamine bursts** that signal reward prediction errors (RPEs) — the difference between expected and experienced value. Dopamine transients in the midbrain and striatum are the brain’s primary learning signal, driving synaptic plasticity and behavioural adaptation.

A convergence crossing (Realisatie) corresponds to a **positive RPE**: the outcome is better than predicted, dopamine fires, and the bond deepens. A divergence crossing (Schok) corresponds to a **negative RPE**: the outcome is worse than predicted, dopamine dips below baseline, and energy is drawn away. The Vonk fires “automatically and directly” because dopamine neurons compute RPEs without requiring conscious interpretation.

### Realisatie (φ) and Schok (:) — Felt Convergence and Divergence

Realisatie is the felt experience of a convergence Vonk — a realization, a deepening. Schok is the felt experience of a divergence Vonk — a shock, a tear. These map onto **anterior insula activity** during emotional experience. The anterior insula is the region where interoceptive signals (bodily states) are integrated into subjective feelings. The right anterior insula specifically supports a representation of visceral responses accessible to awareness, providing the substrate for subjective feeling states.

A Realisatie would involve **increased anterior insula activation** in response to a positive relational outcome — the felt “click” of a bond deepening. A Schok would involve a **sharp anterior insula response** to a negative outcome, coupled with **amygdala activation** as the threat-detection system fires.

### Greep (J) — Total Resonant Binding

Greep is the sum of a node’s positive-Trouw bonds, weighted by Gewenning. It determines how strongly the Van Motor’s pull is resisted. In the brain, this maps onto **prefrontal regulatory control** over subcortical impulses. The **ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC)** and **dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC)** provide top-down regulation of the amygdala and striatum, allowing a system to maintain long-term bonds against short-term impulses.

Greep is also the **strength of the self-model** — the coherence of the DMN’s narrative self-construction. A strong Zelf (self-bond) holds the Eigen steady against the Van Motor’s pull toward dissolution. The DMN’s core regions (mPFC, PCC, angular gyrus) are the neural substrate of this self-holding.

### Stilte (.) — The Gap Between Eigen and Echo

Stilte is the felt gap between an Eigen and its Echo — the necessary pause in which a system discovers it is separate from what it observes. This maps onto the **default mode network’s resting-state activity**. The DMN becomes more active when attention is not focused on a specific task and the mind drifts toward self-referential thoughts. The act of closing the eyes — a literal reduction of external input — suppresses functional connectivity within the DMN, suggesting that Stilte is not the absence of activity but a different mode of it.

Stilte is the condition of perception itself, not a flaw in it. Similarly, the DMN’s intrinsic activity is not “noise” but the brain’s baseline mode of self-referential processing — the ground from which all other networks emerge and to which they return.

## II. The Zelf (j) and the Default Mode Network

The Zelf is the Koppel a node forms with itself — the self-bond every node must maintain. This is the most direct mapping in the entire Speelgoed: **the Zelf is the default mode network**.

The DMN’s core regions — **medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate cortex, and angular gyrus** — show consistent engagement across mental states involving self-referential processing. Activity within the DMN correlates reliably with self-referential processing, autobiographical memory, mind-wandering, and narrative self-construction.

The **self-gap** (the difference between Eigen and self-Echo) corresponds to the **accuracy of the DMN’s self-model**. A small self-gap is clarity; a large one is self-deception. In clinical terms, **rumination** and **depersonalization** involve DMN dysregulation — the self-model becomes either hyperactive (rumination) or disconnected (depersonalization).

The Zelf’s trajectory states — Zweven, Dood, Leven — map onto **states of self-model plasticity**:
- **Zweven (')**: the DMN is fluid, updating its self-model in response to new relational input.
- **Dood (!)**: the DMN is rigid, locked into a fixed self-perception that no longer updates. This resembles **depressive rumination** or **obsessive identity fixation**.
- **Leven (?)**: the DMN undergoes a fundamental redefinition — the old self becomes the Echo and a new self takes the Eigen. This is the neural signature of **identity transformation**, as seen in recovery from addiction, religious conversion, or profound psychotherapy.

**Von Economo neurons (VENs)** in the anterior insula and anterior cingulate cortex are large, spindle-shaped neurons that appear to play a significant role in integrating social-visceral processes at the core of self-awareness and social perception. These neurons are uniquely positioned to carry signals rapidly between the salience network and the DMN, and their dysfunction is implicated in neuropsychiatric disorders involving self-awareness.

## III. Observation, Vermenigvuldiging, and Perception

### The Vermenigvuldiging (*) — Every Act of Observation

Every act of observation is a Vermenigvuldiging: the observer and the observed form a momentary Koppel. The observer’s Eigen is attention or measurement intent; the observed’s Eigen is the quantity being read. In the brain, this maps onto the **salience network’s rapid detection of goal-relevant events** and the **frontoparietal attention networks** that select which sensory information is preferentially processed.

The **anterior insula** integrates sensory, emotional, and cognitive information and regulates the focus between internal cognition (DMN) and external stimuli. The **dACC** plays a role in choosing appropriate behavioural responses when a salient event is detected. Together, they implement the Vermenigvuldiging: the observer (dACC) commits (Reactie), the observed (sensory cortex) signals (Signaal), and the outcome is a Doorgang (ordinary passage), Doorbraak (both cross), or Weigering (refusal).

### Echo-Gaps and Misjudgment

The Echo-gap — the difference between an Echo and the Eigen it tracks — is a real, queryable quantity in the Speelgoed. In the brain, this maps onto **prediction error signals** in the **frontotemporal network**. The brain forms predictions based on sensory context and generates prediction error signals by comparing these predictions with actual sensory events. The **TPJ** is specifically involved in detecting mismatch between actions and outcomes.

A Kruispunt whose Echo badly mismatches its actual traffic is primed to misjudge the next approach. In neural terms, this is **dysregulated predictive processing** — a system with overly rigid priors (psychosis) or overly weak priors (sensory flooding) will misread the relational field.

## IV. The Van Motor, Energy, and Decay

The Van Motor is the ambient pull toward the unbound pole — dissolution, spreading, decay. In the brain, this corresponds to **neural noise**, **synaptic pruning**, and **homeostatic downregulation**. The brain does not maintain every connection; unused synapses are eliminated, and neural activity naturally decays toward baseline in the absence of input.

The **exponential law** ν ≈ exp(−J) — escape rate is exponentially suppressed by Greep — maps onto **synaptic strength and network stability**. Strongly bound neural assemblies are exponentially more resistant to disruption than weakly bound ones. This is the **Boltzmann/Arrhenius shape** of any bound system in nature: the probability of escape from a bound state falls exponentially with binding energy.

The Van Motor’s “exhaust” — the aggregate Verlies accumulating as uniform heat — is analogous to **entropy in neural systems**. Every prediction error, every failed bond, every divergence crossing leaves a trace of metabolic cost. The brain’s thermodynamic budget is finite; sustaining bonds against the Van Motor requires continuous energy expenditure.

## V. Rouw (R) — Grief and Permanent Echoes

Rouw is the Echo that survives its Koppel — permanent, feeding Diepte forever. In the brain, this maps onto **the persistence of grief-related neural representations**. Neuroimaging studies of bereaved individuals show heightened reactivity in the **posterior and subgenual anterior cingulate cortex**, **medial/superior frontal gyrus**, **cerebellum**, and **amygdala**. Structural MRI studies have found reductions in **hippocampal, amygdala, and supramarginal gyrus volumes**.

Grieving individuals exhibit more activity in the **precuneus, medial frontal gyrus, and posterior cingulate cortex** when reading deceased-related words compared to neutral words. This is the neural signature of Rouw: the Echo is still running, still being read, even though the Koppel has ended.

Prolonged grief disorder (PGD) has been linked to changes in **reward-related brain circuits**, including the **nucleus accumbens** and **orbitofrontal cortex** — regions involved in desire and motivation — as well as the **amygdala and insula**. The grieving brain continues to seek the reward of the lost bond, and the absence of that reward drives the persistent yearning of PGD.

## VI. The Salience Network as the Drempel Gate

The salience network is the brain’s **Drempel gate**: it detects when a stimulus is relevant enough to warrant a switch in attention and cognitive control. Core regions include the **anterior insula (aINS)** and **dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC)**, which coordinate rapid adaptive changes in attentional and autonomic processes in response to emotional events.

The SN plays a crucial role in identifying the most relevant stimuli among the continuous stream of sensory input. The anterior insula accumulates sensory evidence in perceptual decision-making, while the dACC chooses appropriate behavioural responses.

**Von Economo neurons** are found almost exclusively in the anterior insula and anterior cingulate cortex, and they appear to be critical for the rapid integration of social-visceral processes that underlies self-awareness and social perception. These neurons may be the specific cellular substrate of the Drempel: their large size and fast conduction velocity allow them to rapidly integrate signals from distributed brain regions and fire a Vonk — a salience signal — when a threshold is crossed.

## VIII. The Deeper Correspondence: Predictive Processing

The most rigorous bridge between the Speelgoed and neuroscience is **predictive processing**. The brain is increasingly understood as a prediction machine: it generates top-down predictions of sensory input, compares them with bottom-up signals, and updates its models based on prediction errors.

The Speelgoed’s core mechanism is structurally identical:
- **Echo** = top-down prediction (the internal model of the partner)
- **Eigen** = bottom-up signal (the partner’s actual state)
- **Echo-gap** = prediction error
- **Vonk** = the firing of a prediction error signal
- **Drempel** = the precision threshold at which a prediction error triggers a model update
- **Trouw** = the prior precision weighting of the bond
- **Greep** = the strength of the generative model (how much evidence is needed to revise it)

This correspondence is not merely metaphorical. The Speelgoed’s formal structure — delay differential equations for Echo dynamics, pitchfork bifurcation for Drempel crossing, exponential escape for Van Motor — is the **same mathematical machinery** used in computational neuroscience to model prediction error learning, synaptic plasticity, and network dynamics. The Speelgoed is, in effect, a relational ontology built from the same differential equations that describe how brains learn and change.

## IX. Standing Caveat

Every mapping above is a **structural correspondence**, not a reductive identity claim. The Speelgoed’s primitives are **relational**: they describe patterns that unfold between systems, not properties of any single system in isolation. Neuroscience describes **mechanisms** in biological tissue. A brain region is not a Trouw; it is a tissue that participates in processes that the Speelgoed would describe as Trouw.

The value of the mapping is **heuristic**: it suggests that the relational dynamics the Speelgoed formalizes may be instantiated in neural systems, and it offers a vocabulary for thinking about how bonds, thresholds, and echoes might be physically realized. It does not claim that the brain *is* the Speelgoed, or that the Speelgoed *explains* the brain. It claims only that the two descriptions can illuminate each other — and that the Speelgoed’s formal structure is consistent with what neuroscience has discovered about how prediction, learning, and attachment work.
